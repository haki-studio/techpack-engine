# Pro reference: how a professional digitiser builds embroidery, per area

Generated by `engine/proprofile.py` (21 Oct 2026). Numbers: `engine/digitize/rules/pro_reference.json`.
**PROVISIONAL**: 10 designs from one digitiser, all small (covered area 20–85 cm², at most 11 cm across).
Re-run and revisit every threshold below when the reference set reaches ~50 files, and before anything is set for a
jacket back: no large-format pro file has been measured yet.

## Why

Our engine extrapolates ~890k stitches for the full Castle back (~2,800 cm²), about 310 st/cm². The house
reference was 250–350k for a back. The founder asked two things. Is that rate normal? If not, does the extra come
from layering or from detail? The rule we want is that large designs follow the same per-area principles as small pro
files. So everything here is a RATE per cm², and we measure our own files with the same code.

## Reference set (allow-list, hard-coded)

`A-0661, A-0698, C-3078, D-4015, D-4058, D-4063, E-4816, E-5045, E-5056, F-5643`: the professionally digitised John
Deer stock designs (Wilcom/Hatch, `_M` size), read from `PRO_REF_DIR` (default `/mnt/c/Users/devin/My Embroidery/My
Machine Files/`). The other files in that folder (`Law.DST` and the `Chopper Jacket` folder) are the founder's own
amateur work. They are never read, and a test pins this. These designs are licensed: the stitch files and renders are
never committed. Only derived numbers are committed. Renders go to the git-ignored `out/pro_reference/`.

## Native size or auto-resized?

Every pro PES header names its source as `<code> <title>_M.EMB`, for example `D-4063 WOLF STARE_M.EMB`. This is
stored per file as `source_design` in the JSON. So each `_M` file was exported from its own Wilcom object design (EMB)
at size M. It was not a stitch file scaled from another size: Wilcom regenerates stitches from objects when an EMB is
resized, so density and stitch length are set for the size sewn.

The row spacing is also the same across all 10 designs (medians 0.20–0.25 mm). A stitch-level rescale would make it
vary with each design's scale factor.

What the files cannot show is whether the digitiser set 0.20–0.25 mm on purpose, or whether Wilcom's
size-dependent auto-density produced it. We do not have the EMB files to check. It matters because, if it is
auto-density for small sizes, the same digitiser would sew a jacket back at a lighter density. The pro density is
therefore **unmeasured at jacket scale**.

## Method

Everything comes from the stitch file. Geometry and thread colours are read from the PES. Trims and jumps are counted
from the DST with `readback.dst` (LESSONS B8).

1. **Runs**: stitches between trims or jumps, inside each colour block.
2. **Rows**: a run is split wherever the needle turns more than 60°. Two consecutive rows are **linked** when all of
   these hold:
   - they run antiparallel (within 30°);
   - they overlap along their axis;
   - they are 0.08–3 mm apart sideways;
   - the loop they form encloses area. A retrace (a bean / triple run, or a double run) encloses none, so it is never
     linked.

   Up to 2 connector moves are allowed between linked rows. A connector is a turn stitch shorter than 0.6 mm, or a
   side-step across the rows no longer than 3 mm (lattice underlay).
3. **Classes**: a chain of 3 or more linked rows is a hatch object. It is **satin** when it has about 1 stitch per row
   (the thread spans the whole width) and **fill** when rows have several stitches. Everything else is **run**
   material: single runs, beans, travel and ties. A run, or an open hatch (row spacing 0.9 mm or more), is
   **underlay** when 80 % or more of it lies under a dense object sewn later in the same colour block. This covers
   edge, centre, zig-zag and lattice underlay, and also hidden travel under the object's own top.
4. **Coverage**: each segment marks the 0.5 mm cells it passes through, and each object's cells are closed by one
   cell. Covered area is the union of all objects. **Depth** is the number of objects covering a cell. **Dense top**
   depth counts satins and dense fills only.
5. **Identity**, exact by construction, used to explain any rate:

   `st/cm² (covered) = 100 × D × ρ / L`

   - **D**: mean layer depth (sum of object areas / covered area). This is the **layering** factor.
   - **ρ**: thread mm per mm² of object area (about 1 / row spacing). This is the **density** factor.
   - **L**: mean stitch length. This is the **detail** factor: short stitches come from small parts, satins and turns.

**Classifier checks.** Every pro and ours file was rendered with a class overlay (satin red, fill blue, run green,
underlay amber) and a dense-depth map, and these were looked at zoomed in. Stitch-weighted confidence is 0.84–0.93 on
the pro files and 0.75–0.96 on ours. Confidence is the share of an object's row links that agree with its median
spacing, or for a run the share of straight or clean-reversal stitches.

Two defects were found on the renders and fixed, each with a test:
- A bean along a curve was read as a 0.1–0.2 mm "fill". This is fixed by the enclosed-area retrace test.
- Lattice underlay with 2 mm side-steps, and dst2 radial fans, were read as runs. This is fixed by side-step
  connectors.

**Known limits.**
- Overlap widths (how far a satin or fill sits on an earlier fill) are limited by the 0.5 mm raster: anything under
  about 1 mm reads as 1.0 mm. They stay in the JSON but are not quoted here, because the pull-comp and underlap values
  that matter (0.2–1 mm) are below the grid limit.
- There is no text detector. None of the 10 designs has lettering, so "smallest text" is not measured.
- Puff, chenille and chain are classed by geometry only. KCOS moss loops read as run.

## What explains the Castle stitch rate

On the crop, `289 st/cm² = 100 × D 2.66 × ρ 2.02 / L 1.85`. That is about 4.6× an ideal single house tatami layer
(0.45 mm rows × 3.5 mm stitches, about 63 st/cm²). The 4.6× splits into three factors:

| factor | crop | ideal layer | multiplier | where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| layering (D) | 2.66 | 1 | ×2.66 | fill 1.54 + underlay 0.68 + run/travel 0.36 + satin 0.08 |
| detail (L) | 1.85 mm | 3.5 mm | ×1.89 | fills in small parts: the crop's fill stitches have a median of 2.2 mm (house 3.5), with 7.5 objects/cm² |
| density (ρ) | 2.02 | 2.22 | ×0.91 | fills at 0.41 mm rows ≈ the house 0.45 |

The fill share of D (1.54) means the fill alone is 1.5 objects deep on average. That comes from 1 mm underlap bands,
blends laid over fills, and details sewn on fills.

**The pro builds the same way, and more so.**
- The pro rate is `483 = 100 × 3.57 × 2.80 / 2.10`.
- Measured against each one's own single fill layer, the rate multiplier is the same: the pro sits at 483 / 178 =
  **2.7×** one pro fill layer, and the crop at 289 / 110 = **2.6×** one crop fill layer.
- Pro fill layer: 0.215 mm rows × 2.62 mm stitches = 178 st/cm². Crop fill layer: 0.41 × 2.2 = 110 st/cm².

So "about 3× one fill layer" is **consistent with small-design pro practice**. It is not an engine defect. It is
**unmeasured at jacket scale**: no large-format pro file is in the set.

Where we differ from the pro, in order of size:
1. **Density per layer.** Pro fill row spacing is about half of ours: 0.20–0.25 mm against our 0.39–0.44 mm. Thread
   per layer area (ρ, which includes satins, runs and edges) is 1.4× ours: 2.80 against 2.02 mm/mm².
2. **Layering.** The pro is more layered: D 3.6 against our 2.7. Dense top layers are 2 or more deep on 74 % of the
   pro area (ours 48 %) and 3 or more deep on 37 % (ours 12 %). The pro lays a base fill, then narrow satin detail
   (fur, outlines) over it, then an outline last.
3. **Detail.** The pro has similar or slightly longer mean stitches and more objects per cm² (11.8 against 7.5).

Our ~890k for the back (~318 st/cm²) is below the per-area rate of every small pro design here (min 356 st/cm²). The
old 250–350k back budget works out to about 90–125 st/cm². No pro file comes close to that, and it is below even one
pro fill layer (178 st/cm²). Small-design rates are not an estimate for a back, because a digitiser may sew large areas
lighter. They are only an upper reference from small designs.

Caveat: these are small designs. A digitiser may well sew a jacket back lighter, for hand and run time on 12 oz duck.
That is unmeasured until we get large-format pro files.

## Proposed thresholds and engine changes (ranked; PROPOSED, nothing changed in this PR)

1. **PROPOSED: report the per-area rate, not an absolute back budget.**
   - Report st/cm² of covered area with D, ρ and L for every file, on the G pages and the bench.
   - Mark any of the four that falls outside the pro p10–p90 band, **in either direction**, as "review" (not fail).
     Today every full-coverage file of ours is below the band on rate, D and ρ.
   - Say plainly that the over-dense limit for a jacket back is **unknown**. These small designs give no ceiling for
     a back, so no "too many stitches" gate should be set from them.
   - Drop 250–350k as a reference for full-coverage scenes; it implies about 100 st/cm².
2. **PROPOSED: trims gate from the pro.** `bench.json trims_per_100cm2_max` is 25, below the pro median of 31.4
   (p10–p90: 6–52). Set the max to 50 (≈ pro p90) and keep 31 as a target. The crop (37.6) would pass the max.
3. **PROPOSED: rule 6 re-based on layering.**
   - Every pro file stacks dense layers 3 or more deep over 12–66 % of its area (median 37 %), as narrow satin detail
     on a base fill.
   - Change "never more than 2 stacked" to "at most 2 stacked FILLS; satin or run detail ≤ ~2.5 mm wide may lie on
     top".
   - Have the `part_stack` gate exempt that detail.
   - Needs the founder and a strike-off on duck before it changes anything.
4. **PROPOSED: detail last.** The pro sews outlines and detail last in 10 of 10 files, and 21–38 % of its stitches are
   satin. The crop's satin share is 5 %, and its last block is 7 % satin or run. Add a sequencing preference ("line and
   satin detail blocks last") and report the last-block satin+run share as a fidelity indicator, not a gate.
5. **PROPOSED: underlay + hidden same-colour travel ≤ pro p90 (19 % of stitches).** The crop is at 20.6 %. The excess
   is travel hidden under its own fill (LESSONS B35).
6. **PROPOSED: no change to fill density without a strike-off.** Pro fills at 0.21 mm come from small designs. Sew a
   duck swatch at 0.30, 0.40 and 0.45 mm and set the fill-spacing band from that, not from this table.
7. **Confirmed, no change needed:**
   - Satin minimum width 1.0 mm matches the pro p5 of 0.98. The thinnest pro columns are 0.6–1.0 mm.
   - Satin same-side spacing: ours 0.40–0.46, pro 0.38–0.50.
   - Max stitch: ours 7.4 mm, below the pro p90 of 8.7.


To add pro files:
1. Add their codes to `PRO_ALLOW`. Never glob the folder.
2. Re-run, and look at the `_classes.png` overlays.
3. Update this document's table.
